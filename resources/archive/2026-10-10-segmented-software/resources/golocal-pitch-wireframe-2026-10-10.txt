GoLocal pitch structure — October 10, 2026

BUSINESS
GoLocal is the shared data and booking layer for home services. It recruits participating suppliers, connects their authorized business software, and gives personal agents one endpoint to compare providers, request quotes, check schedules, book, pay and manage jobs. These are proposed capabilities. Proposed revenue: a supplier-paid fee on a fulfilled job from a new customer, with a share for the partner bringing demand.

Headline: $1.2 trillion+ U.S. local-services opportunity (founder-requested wording).
Each new platform unlocks another step. The transaction gap remains.

- 2000–2010: Internet / Google and Craigslist.
- 2010–2020: Mobile + GPS / Google Maps.
- 2020–2025: LLMs / AI chatbots.

2. Compare options: internet onward.
3. Location and reviews: Maps onward.
4. Conversational request: chatbots onward.

Dark-green diagonal marks the new unlock; pale green carries earlier gains forward. The explanatory green/red legend is removed. Three bottom rows remain red: scope/quote, live availability, book/pay. Final agent cells identify calls, calls/sites and sites/checkout as external work still needed. Red means manual/external work, not impossibility or absence of all digital transaction providers.

Market basis unchanged: mixed-year indicative annual U.S. spending across selected local-service categories, including materials, not GoLocal fee revenue. Historical progression illustrative, not precise launch dates or a platform feature audit. No product introduction on slide 1.

3. THE CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE
Headline: A customer request becomes a confirmed booking.
Customer request: “Book a plumber in Dallas tomorrow morning for my leaking kitchen tap.”
Native editable four-step timeline with explicit ownership:
1. Personal agent scopes the job: collects address and a photo, defines a diagnostic visit.
2. GoLocal checks live options: matches connected providers, returns available slots and visit fees.
3. Customer and agent approve the visit: choose illustrative Oak Plumbing, approve $79 visit and payment authorization.
4. GoLocal confirms the booking: books in the provider's system, returns booking ID and payment status.
Final customer confirmation: Oak Plumbing tomorrow 9–11 am, booking GL-1042, $79 visit fee authorized. Repairs quoted after inspection.
This is an illustrative proposed journey, not production traction or a real quote. Names, fees, slots and ID are examples. Provider acceptance establishes confirmation; availability is rechecked before commitment. Diagnostic visit fee does not cover repair. Payment authorization is distinct from settlement. Supported booking and payment operations require merchant and customer authorization.
Slide 3 no longer repeats slide 2's ecosystem diagram or unmeasured speed comparison.
