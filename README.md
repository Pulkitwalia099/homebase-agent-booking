# Homebase

Current project context · 2026-10-05

Homebase is exploring a service that lets consumer personal agents find participating local businesses and obtain reliable bookings through the businesses' existing software.

## Current working position

Investigate whether an agent company such as Wajo would use a supplier network operated by Homebase. Homebase would recruit willing cleaners, agree bookable services and terms, connect their software, and handle booking problems. A connector alone may not justify an independent business.

The leading commercial hypothesis is a cleaner-funded success fee for a completed, paid, new-to-provider customer, shared with the agent company. The illustrated $60 fee split into $36 for Wajo and $24 for Homebase is an assumption, not an agreed price. Homebase must provide enough supply or remove enough work to justify its share relative to Wajo integrating directly.

Boston-area first standard residential cleans are a provisional pilot. Choose the service area and integration from real agent demand and willing cleaners. Public examples identify Launch27 and BookingKoala; Jobber, Housecall Pro and MaidCentral are other candidates to investigate when a participating cleaner uses them.

## Next decisions

1. Ask Wajo or another agent company for recent relevant requests, its current completion process, and what it would buy rather than build.
2. Ask cleaners about desired jobs, capacity, scope and price rules, acquisition economics, and willingness to pay a success fee.
3. With an interested cleaner, verify its software vendor's access, supported booking operations and commercial permissions.
4. Agree one narrow pilot and fee split, process genuine requests, reconcile outcomes and costs, then seek paid continuation.

Demand, willingness to pay, production access, profitable delivery and venture scale remain unproven. Keep operator capacity recovery and specific property-maintenance coordination problems as adjacent hypotheses.
